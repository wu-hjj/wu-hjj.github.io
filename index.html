I am a mathematician interested in how combinatorial structure helps us understand complex systems and makes difficult optimization problems tractable.

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Networks & Optimization group at CWI in Amsterdam, working with Monique Laurent.

I received my PhD from ETH Zürich in October 2025, supervised by Ulrik Brandes, with Afonso Bandeira as my second advisor.

Research

My work focuses on the relationship between graph structure and the optimization landscape of synchronization problems. I look for combinatorial structures that make nonconvex problems tractable, using tools from combinatorics, graph theory, low-rank semidefinite optimization, and dynamical systems.

A central theme is how local structural constraints can lead to global synchronization. My recent work studies this question through threshold graphs and graph skeletons.

More broadly, I am interested in mathematical explanations of collective behavior in natural and social systems, including their resilience and robustness.

Publications & preprints

Synchronization & nonconvex optimization

  1. 2025Preprint

    Benign nonconvexity of synchronization landscape induced by graph skeletons

    Hongjin Wu, Ulrik Brandes

    Submitted · arXiv:2512.21935

    arXivPDF
    Research summary

    We establish global synchronization for connected quasi-threshold graphs through a structural argument based on rooted-tree skeletons. Synchronization propagates from the leaves toward the root, linking local optimality conditions to the global landscape.

  2. 2025Preprint

    Global synchronization beyond dense graphs: the case of threshold graphs

    Hongjin Wu, Ulrik Brandes

    Submitted · arXiv:2511.12646

    arXivPDF
    Research summary

    We show that connected threshold graphs are second-order globally synchronizing. Their recursive structure provides a route to synchronization that does not require the graphs to be dense or have large minimum degree.
